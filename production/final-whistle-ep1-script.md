# Final Whistle Stories — Episode 1: Barcelona, 1999

[COLD OPEN]
Welcome to Final Whistle Stories.
Every match has a final whistle.
But once in a very long while, the story isn't over when you think it is.
Camp Nou, Barcelona. The twenty-sixth of May, nineteen ninety-nine.
Ninety minutes on the clock. One goal on the scoreboard.
And Manchester United were losing the Champions League final.

[THE ROAD HERE]
That season, United had already won the league and the FA Cup.
One more trophy would make them the first English club to win all three in a single season.
The Treble.
But the night did not begin the way a historic night should.
Roy Keane and Paul Scholes, the heart of the midfield, were suspended.
And in the sixth minute, Bayern Munich scored from a free kick.
One-nil.

[THE LONG WAIT]
What followed was not a fight. It was a siege that never quite broke through.
Bayern had the control, and they had the chances.
They hit the post. They hit the crossbar.
Each time, the German supporters in the stadium were sure they had seen the end of it.
Alex Ferguson looked at the clock and made his changes.
Teddy Sheringham came on in the sixty-seventh minute.
Ole Gunnar Solskjaer came on in the eighty-first.
Neither was in the starting eleven.
Neither seemed to be the answer.
The fourth official held up the board.
Three minutes of added time.

[NINETY PLUS ONE]
A corner. A scramble. A loose ball at the edge of the area.
Ryan Giggs swung at it and got it wrong.
It dribbled off his boot, and Sheringham was right there to turn it into the net.
One-all.
The United fans behind the goal went silent for half a second.
And then the noise came.
Barely a minute later, United won another corner.

[NINETY PLUS THREE]
David Beckham stepped up.
He had been the smallest part of the story all night, and now he was the whole of it.
The ball came in. Sheringham got a head to it and sent it across the face of goal.
And there was Solskjaer.
He stuck out a foot. The ball hit the roof of the net.
Two-one.
Ninety-one minutes to ninety-three.
Two goals in the time it takes most people to finish a coffee.

[THE WHISTLE]
Bayern's players collapsed onto the grass.
Some of them couldn't stand.
The referee blew the final whistle, and one of the greatest comebacks the competition has ever seen was complete.
Manchester United had not played the better football.
They had simply refused to stop playing.

[CLOSE]
Sport gives us a lot of stories about talent.
This one is about timing, and about the thin line between over and not over.
Bayern were so close to the trophy that they could see their own colors on it.
And then the clock ran out. Just not for them.
