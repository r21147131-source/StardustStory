THE ODYSSEY — How a 3,000-year-old story became the movie event of 2026
Recording script as voiced; sections separated by '—— ——'.


What if the biggest movie of 2026 wasn't based on a comic book...

Or a video game...

Or a bestselling novel from the last twenty years?

What if it was a story that's been around for roughly 3,000 years?

Because that's exactly what Christopher Nolan has done with The Odyssey.

A story first told by Homer.

A story about gods, monsters, war, temptation, revenge, loyalty and one man's desperate attempt to get home.

And somehow...

Christopher Nolan has taken that ancient story and turned it into one of the biggest theatrical events of the year.

—— ——

Let's start with something almost unbelievable.

The Odyssey isn't a new story.

In fact, it's one of the foundational works of Western literature.

The basic premise is simple.

Odysseus has fought in the Trojan War.

The war is over.

Now he wants to go home.

That's it.

But getting home turns into a journey that takes years.

And along the way, Odysseus encounters gods, monsters, strange islands, supernatural forces and people who want to stop him from reaching his destination.

There's the Cyclops.

There's Circe.

There's the Sirens.

There's Calypso.

There's Poseidon.

And back home in Ithaca, his wife Penelope is waiting while an entire group of suitors tries to take Odysseus' place.

His son, Telemachus, is also growing up without his father.

So underneath all the monsters and mythology...

The Odyssey is actually a story about home.

And that's probably one of the reasons it has survived for thousands of years.

Because the basic idea is still incredibly easy to understand.

You can leave home.

You can become someone else.

You can experience incredible things.

But eventually...

you want to know where you belong.

And then Christopher Nolan came along.

Nolan is one of the few directors working today who can turn a movie into an event simply by announcing that he's making it.

After Oppenheimer, he chose one of the most ambitious stories imaginable.

Homer's Odyssey.

And the cast immediately made people pay attention.

Matt Damon plays Odysseus.

Tom Holland plays Telemachus.

Anne Hathaway plays Penelope.

Zendaya appears as Athena.

Robert Pattinson is part of the ensemble.

Charlize Theron plays Calypso.

And that's only part of the cast.

But the cast isn't actually the craziest thing about this movie.

The craziest thing is how Nolan decided to film it.

—— ——

Nolan didn't approach The Odyssey like a conventional fantasy movie.

He wanted it to feel enormous.

So the film was shot entirely using IMAX film cameras.

And that is a big deal.

Because IMAX isn't just a larger screen.

The format is designed to give audiences an enormous image.

More information.

More scale.

More physical presence.

And Nolan has spent years experimenting with large-format filmmaking.

But The Odyssey pushed that philosophy even further.

IMAX developed new technology for the production, and the movie became the first theatrical release shot entirely with IMAX film cameras.

Think about what that means.

You're taking a story that was originally communicated through poetry...

and transforming it into one of the largest images modern cinema can create.

That's the central contradiction of The Odyssey.

It's incredibly old...

but it looks incredibly modern.

—— ——

And audiences clearly responded.

The movie opened worldwide to approximately $263.8 million, making it Nolan's biggest opening ever.

But the IMAX numbers are arguably even more interesting.

Within its first month, The Odyssey had generated approximately $289 million from IMAX screenings, making it the highest-grossing IMAX release at that point.

That tells us something important.

People weren't simply interested in watching The Odyssey.

They were interested in experiencing it.

And that's exactly what Nolan has been building toward throughout his career.

—— ——
